## What this batch teaches

1. **The Second Serve You Let Escape** (`return-time`): take an available early
   contact before adding pace; compare arrival clocks; give ground when the early
   low ball is genuinely unreachable from this start.
2. **The Safe Second Serve Trap** (`serve-quality`): a legal landing is only the
   beginning; observe the receiver's contact; change the construction when a
   different receiver is comfortable with height. This is not a technique diagnosis.
3. **One Point Is a Clue** (`pattern-clue`): retain the incoming-shot conditions
   with the remembered reply; compare like contacts; do not carry a lob expectation
   into a materially different contact. This is not a fixed repetition threshold.

Each lesson has one insight, three connected decisions and twelve authored choice
branches. The shared coaching card and attention policy remain in use. The prior
18 serialized lesson definitions exactly match the pre-edit SHA-256 snapshot:

- Return: the early return arrives at 2.15s; the later, genuinely faster return at
  2.36s. The earlier stroke does not falsely claim to shorten both flights. The
  boundary derives the reachable contact rather than placing a ghost on an
  unreachable ball: early forward contact is 1.92m high; giving ground provides a
  1.38m descending contact. A different start can reverse the conclusion.
- Serve: all transfer alternatives now use the same 0.18s reaction and 2.7m/s
  movement budget. The wide delivery genuinely leaves 1.078m reach. Previously a
  different alternative implicitly allowed enough speed to erase that distinction.
  Read wording describes observed receiving contacts, not forced retreat.
- Pattern: history is revealed in order; reply icons wait for the observed reply.
  The transfer ends before the outgoing reply, without pre-playing the correct
  preparation. Clipped lob geometry and reversed thumbnail depth were corrected.
