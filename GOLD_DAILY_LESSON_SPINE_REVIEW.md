## Three-lesson batch — 13 September 2026

These entries supersede their research-only rows below. They have no invented Predict connection; each uses its exact existing Sharpen region.

- **Running Around Is Not Hiding**: backhand construction → deeper ball removes the circle → a reached forehand still needs to pay for recovery. Barty’s coach Craig Tyzzer described her slice setting up her forehand weapon: the backhand helped build the attack rather than merely survive.
- **The Return That Shrinks Serve +1**: deep middle neutralises → short middle does not → a softer second serve earns width. De Minaur sent most first-serve returns through the middle in 2024, reducing sideline risk and denying the server an immediate attacking angle.
- **The Open Court Is Not Always Open**: moving defender → stationary defender reverses target → opposite wing and movement require a fresh read. At Indian Wells, Jankovic’s coach Chip Brooks urged her to hit behind Kleybanova: running into the open court was helping her opponent attack.

Primary evidence: [Tyzzer interview](https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2465400/coaches-corner-bartys-best-is-yet-to-come-tyzzer), [ATP return analysis](https://www.atptour.com/en/news/de-minaur-infosys-atp-beyond-the-numbers-december-2024), and [contemporaneous Jankovic match report](https://www.tennisserver.com/photofeed/2010/100318-bnp_paribas_open.shtml). Barty evidence supports the construction, not every simulated run-around choice; Jankovic is a reported coaching exchange, not a claim that every ball behind wins. GameSharp’s applications remain interpretations. The animated scenarios are authored examples, not reconstructions of those professional matches.

## Living decision-stage pass — 5 September 2026

The three playable prototypes now share one stable mobile interaction stage. The visual arrives first, the three observable cues resolve on the same authored clock as the court action, and the question remains immediately available. Answering preserves the exact reading position; advancing deliberately resets to the top of the next decision.

- Recovery now shows the shot, the opponent displacement and the resulting recovery choices rather than presenting a finished diagram.
- Short Ball now shows the incoming ball and the player moving into the authored contact state before asking for the decision.
- Contact keeps the validated moving forehand and settles on the same contact frame used by its explanation.
- Wrong choices show the selected consequence before the higher-percentage correction; correct choices show only the earned consequence.
- Every stage uses the same replay control and only recorded ground-stroke, slice, bounce and shoe audio. No success/failure tone exists.
- The short “coach ink” inside the evidence stage supplies the emotional payoff; the causal sentence and optional **Why?** retain the learning muscle without forcing a paragraph before the decision.

The implementation was manually replayed at 320 × 568 and 430 × 932, across all nine decisions, including wrong answers, replay, tap-to-finish, continuation and all three result screens. At 430px the decision stage fits without scrolling. At 320px, preserving readable two-line choices and 44px-plus tap targets requires a small scroll for the lowest choice on some interactions; shrinking the court or controls further was rejected as a worse usability trade.

Two defects were found only in the visual pass and corrected:

- Contact’s written payoff initially covered its instruction line. Contact now reserves its own non-overlapping payoff row.
- The first tap-to-finish implementation ended child animations independently, which could erase the shot line and strand a player mid-movement. A skip now seeks the entire authored scene clock to its final evidence state; contact settles on the exact diagnosis frame or comparison.

Reduced motion fails to a complete evidence frame, removes all decorative transitions and suppresses audio whose timing would no longer match motion. The ordinary app path remains unchanged: these files still load only behind `?goldDaily=…`, and production eligibility remains zero.

## Contact-pilot evidence reset — 6 September 2026

The third prototype was narrowed after a hostile evidence review. Its earlier sequence asked the player to infer late timing, preparation and footwork from an animation that could only defend one visible fact: the ball was crowded close to the player at contact.

The revised sequence now teaches one bounded skill:

1. observe the crowded body-ball relationship without naming a cause;
2. compare it with more usable spacing from the same camera;
3. recognise the same clue under a different ball outcome, then check whether it repeats on court.

The browser replay caught three additional issues after the first green test run:

- the shared renderer called the comparison **MISS / FIX**, contradicting the lesson’s evidence boundary; the pilot now says **CROWDED / REFERENCE** and does not imply the reference guarantees an outcome;
- the transfer initially asked what was “worth checking,” leaving several defensible answers; it now asks which earlier visible clue appears again;
- reduced motion could leave a repeating contact pulse and wait on the normal comparison timer; it now renders a complete static comparison immediately.

The contract now rejects contact content that merges observation with diagnosis, omits an uncertainty boundary, changes the crowded-spacing clue between decisions, fails to author the exact pre-answer visual state or restores causal comparison labels. The pilot remains withheld from production pending qualified coaching review and real-player ambiguity testing.

## Slice-serve geometry lock — 9 September 2026

The Serve + First Ball prototype now explicitly authors one perspective: camera behind a right-handed near-baseline server on the deuce court. The server begins screen-right of centre, the slice lands in the far-left service box, the returner moves just outside the screen-left singles sideline and the ball continues screen-left after the bounce.

Flight and post-bounce skid are rendered as separate paths with a visible bounce beat and a 300ms hold before the return begins. This prevents a smooth curve—or the following return—from making the serve appear to reverse direction.

The contract now rejects any serve-plus-one step that changes the handedness, court side, spin, camera orientation, diagonal service box, displaced returner position or leftward post-bounce exit independently. A single smoothed path across this spin-defined bounce is also forbidden.
