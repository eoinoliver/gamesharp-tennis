# Movement as a decision — beta candidate, 26 October 2026

Three new lessons, nine decisions, appended after the established daily order:

| Journey | Pair | Transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Have You Earned the Attack? | Same forehand ball and opening; prepared arrival versus still crossing | Wide ball, central opponent: buy recovery time |
| Defence Can Change Before You Hit | Same wide ball and open line; travelling versus prepared | Other wing, still moving wide |
| Have You Earned the Net? | Same short ball/deep opponent; prepared versus still chasing | Prepared backhand, but low ball: lift the approach |

Users choose a tennis plan. There are no movement controls, reaction-time scores,
or prescriptive step counts. Both members of each pair have identical ball and
opponent samples. The camera is fixed to the same preparation position, so
following the player cannot hide travel. Each lesson finishes with an unlabelled
transfer situation. No BALANCED/LATE/EARLY answer hints appear before commitment.

After commitment, a trace of the actual rendered hip route and circles at the
actual rendered ankles reveal the evidence. The second read offers both arrivals.
Comparison, replay and Why cannot change the first answer. Sound on these replays
is suppressed; existing explicit opt-in and sparse sound policy remain intact.

## Content truth and limits

These are authored decision freezes, ending before the outgoing shot. There are
no invented winners, misses, percentages, future contacts or guaranteed replies.
Existing licensed incoming flight/clip data supply the illustration; only your
start/arrival is adapted. `rally/build_readiness.py` records the derivation.
Tennis-MoCap attribution and license notices remain intact. New full-stroke
biomechanics are deliberately not asserted by these pre-shot illustrations.

The prepared cases accept the sensible safer reply too. Preparation opens an
option, rather than obliging attack. The net transfer adds the low-ball constraint
so that readiness alone is not a universal instruction to attack. A single result
cannot validate a tactical choice.

Inspiration: SweetSpot Tennis's “Make Room to Swing” and “Step Into the Shot”
checkpoint framing, and ACE Tennis Lab's short stroke breakdown format. No video
assets were copied. Titles/selected checkpoints were inspected, not a full
transcript or independent certification of either channel's biomechanics.

This first slice concentrates on arrival and starting position. It does not yet
teach split timing, the choice of first step, or recovery-position decisions in
these new pairs. Existing split-step/recovery lessons remain available.

## Evidence needed to justify expansion

Read events already distinguish lesson, step, first pick and acceptance. Inspect
first-pass choices on the unlabelled third decision separately from completion,
cue saves, usefulness feedback and next-day returns. A safer accepted answer is
not evidence that a user noticed an additional attacking opportunity. Nor are
immediate transfer answers proof of on-court transfer or retention.

The next useful test is whether people can name what changed without the
annotation and recognise it in another point. If they cannot see it at phone size,
improve the example before adding more lessons. Do not claim efficacy from the
browser suite or synthetic movement checks. Physical-device audibility remains
unverified from the separate iOS repair.
